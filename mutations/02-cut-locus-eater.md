# 02 — THE CUT-LOCUS EATER

## Surgery map

- **Governing mathematics:** the cut locus and conjugate points of a metric flowing across a non-orientable Klein-bottle-like surface.
- **Body:** one continuous sheet with no stable distinction between inside and outside.
- **Eyes:** conjugate points where neighboring geodesics refocus.
- **Mouths:** cut-locus branches where equally short paths arrive from incompatible directions.
- **Tongues:** follow competing shortest paths and meet themselves from the wrong side.
- **Reused organs:** sphere eversion, macro photography, hydraulic membranes, glossy black separatrices, thin-film glycerin, optical seams.
- **New metabolism:** the organism feeds on disagreement about distance.

## Master still-image prompt

```text
A claustrophobic macro frame from a damaged VHS copy of a 1984 underground practical-effects film. A single continuous non-orientable membrane fills the stage, constructed as a thick Klein-bottle-like sheet of translucent foam latex repeatedly passing through hidden mirrored seams. It has no stable inside or outside and no conventional creature silhouette. The surface is painted only where necessary; most color comes from saturated practical light passing through stretched rubber.

Across this living metric, glossy black raised ridges mark the true Riemannian cut locus: branching places where multiple equally short geodesics arrive from incompatible directions. Complete wet human mouths tear open only along those cut-locus branches. Each mouth has working lips, gums, teeth, saliva, and an articulated jaw; its two lips are physically opposite sides of the same continuous surface meeting after traveling around the non-orientable body. Cable-operated tongues divide along competing shortest paths, crawl over the membrane, and re-enter the same mouth from the reverse orientation. Realistic acrylic human eyes occur only at conjugate points where neighboring geodesics refocus, their eyelids stretched along curved geodesic arcs and their pupil size controlled by local Gaussian curvature. No facial arrangement and no random anatomy.

The practical prop is a twelve-foot continuous latex sheet running over concealed rollers, mirrored half-silvered joins, hydraulic rams, air bladders, black rubber cut-locus ridges, articulated mouth appliances, and oversized macro eye inserts. Sphere-eversion mechanics periodically push interior tissue through exterior folds. The membrane glows saturated tangerine, cobalt blue, acid lime, electric magenta, and ultraviolet under opposing gelled tungsten banks; wet mica-laced glycerin produces shifting opalescent green and violet at maximum stretch, while deep folds fall into absolute black. A locked 100mm macro lens views the surface as a bodily landscape with no visible room, razor-thin focus on one three-way cut-locus mouth while other geodesic jaws continue into blur.

Ultra-realistic 1980s physical materials: heavy rubber drag, seam tension, cloudy acrylic eyes, sculpted teeth, imperfect cable timing, hot studio light, optical mirror joins, smoke and wetness. Shot on pushed 16mm, transferred to consumer VHS, abandoned in attic heat. Restrained analog decay follows the surface orientation: chroma bleed travels along the geodesic ridges, gate weave makes mirrored seams breathe, brief color-lock failure swaps the apparent inside and outside, mild ghosting leaves one delayed mouth on the wrong side of the membrane. No modern digital glitch.
```

## Image-to-video prompt

```text
Locked 100mm macro camera with shallow depth of field; no camera movement. Concealed rollers slowly pull the continuous non-orientable latex sheet through itself. Hydraulic bladders create a heavy practical sphere-eversion pulse: a fold bulges, turns partially inside out, then becomes the reverse side of the same membrane. Black cut-locus ridges branch only when two geodesic tension waves arrive simultaneously. At each new branch, a complete mechanical human mouth opens, bites once, and seals as the shortest paths separate. Tongues travel along two competing geodesics and return from opposite orientations. Acrylic eyes at conjugate points focus together, then lose coordination as curvature changes. Let tangerine, cobalt, lime, magenta, and ultraviolet thin-film colors slide over the glycerin as the material stretches. Preserve cable stutter, hydraulic weight, mismatched mouth timing, mirror-matte shimmer, 16mm grain, geodesic chroma trails, tape flutter, and brief orientation-reversing ghost images. No liquid CGI or shapeless digital transformation.
```
