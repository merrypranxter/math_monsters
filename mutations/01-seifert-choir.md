# 01 — THE SEIFERT CHOIR

## Surgery map

- **Governing mathematics:** a Seifert-fibered three-manifold with Hopf-linked regular fibers and a small number of exceptional fibers.
- **Body:** not a central creature, but a volume completely threaded by non-intersecting living fibers.
- **Eyes:** open only where exceptional fibers pierce the visible cross-section; gaze direction follows local fiber orientation.
- **Mouths:** torus-knot cores where many regular fibers wind around one singular axis.
- **Tongues:** cable-operated bundles that travel along the fibration without crossing.
- **Reused organs:** translucent latex, acrylic eyes, black structural boundaries, acidic internal light, optical multiplication, VHS chroma delay.
- **New metabolism:** the creature breathes by redistributing twist. A mouth cannot open unless every surrounding fiber compensates.

## Master still-image prompt

```text
A recovered frame from a lost 1986 experimental fantasy-science-fiction body-horror film: an impossible Seifert-fibered organism physically constructed as a room-sized practical effect. There is no head, torso, or central silhouette. The entire frame is filled by hundreds of wet translucent foam-latex tubes and hot-melt vinyl membranes forming a Seifert fibration, every regular fiber looping through the volume without intersecting any other fiber, winding in Hopf-linked families around several thick exceptional cores. The mathematical fibers are the anatomy, not decorative cables attached to a monster.

At each exceptional fiber, the surrounding tubes compress into a complete functional human mouth with wet lips, gums, sculpted teeth, and a cable-operated tongue wound into a torus knot; the mouth exists exactly where the fibration becomes singular. Numerous realistic acrylic human eyes open only where exceptional fibers puncture the camera-facing cross-section, with eyelids following the local helical geodesics, pupils aimed tangentially along the fiber flow, tear-film highlights and asynchronous mechanical blinking. The regular fibers subtly bulge as twist-pressure moves through them, implying that every mouth, eye, and distant loop belongs to one continuous higher-dimensional digestive system.

The physical build is visible and convincing: rotating aluminum ring armatures, translucent latex hoses, concealed aircraft cables, pneumatic bladders, replacement mouth inserts, tiny mechanical acrylic eyes, gallons of glycerin, and multiple optical-printer passes extending the fiber bundles beyond the soundstage. Aggressive but controlled color: internal tungsten backlighting makes the fibers glow electric cyan, bubblegum magenta, toxic chartreuse, and ultraviolet violet, divided by deep black rubber cores; hard white frontal light catches saliva, teeth, and wet acrylic eyes. Static 50mm camera at human chest height, slightly off-axis so the linked fibers create impossible depth rather than a centered portrait, dense saturated smoke caught between the loops.

Ultra-realistic documentation of a hand-built 1980s creature effect photographed on 16mm, then optically multiplied, transferred to consumer VHS, and baked for forty years in a humid attic. Film grain remains beneath video noise. Restrained gate weave, soft telecine detail, magenta/cyan chroma delay curling away from the moving fibers, mild tracking skew, one glowing matte seam around an optically repeated loop, RF dropout near the bottom. The viewer understands the pulleys, rubber, and optical printer, but cannot understand how the linked anatomy occupies the room.
```

## Image-to-video prompt

```text
Locked static camera; do not orbit, dolly, or perform a digital fly-through. The practical Seifert-fiber bundle rotates internally on several hidden ring armatures at slightly different mechanical speeds. Twist-pressure travels through the translucent latex tubes as visible pneumatic bulges. When one exceptional-fiber mouth opens, every surrounding regular fiber tightens and redistributes its twist; distant mouths answer in a slow quasiperiodic choir. Torus-knot tongues extend along their own fiber paths without crossing neighboring tongues. Acrylic eyes blink in coupled clusters and roll tangentially along the local helical flow. Internal cyan, magenta, chartreuse, and violet light breathes through the latex while hard white highlights remain fixed. Preserve heavy rubber inertia, cable hesitation, imperfect lip synchronization, optical matte shimmer, 16mm gate weave, VHS chroma lag, intermittent horizontal tape stretch, and bottom-edge head-switching noise. No smooth morphing; all motion is rotating armature, pneumatics, cable puppetry, or period optical compositing.
```
