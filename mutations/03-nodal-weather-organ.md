# 03 — THE NODAL WEATHER ORGAN

## Surgery map

- **Governing mathematics:** time-varying eigenfunctions on a stretched membrane, coupled through a nonlinear oscillator field.
- **Body:** a room-sized membrane whose visible organs precipitate at zero sets rather than existing continuously.
- **Eyes:** intersections of nodal lines.
- **Mouths:** saddle points where positive and negative vibration domains exchange stability.
- **Tongues:** trace moving antinodal trajectories.
- **Reused organs:** gyroid breathing, stroboscopic motion, CMYK pigment, cyan smoke, mechanical eyes, optical feedback.
- **New metabolism:** anatomy is weather produced by vibration; silence temporarily kills the creature.

## Master still-image prompt

```text
A frame from a lost 1987 experimental body-horror effects test, shot practically on an overheated soundstage and surviving only on attic-damaged VHS. The organism is a room-sized wet latex membrane stretched over a deep irregular frame, driven by an array of hidden industrial speaker cones and mechanical exciters. It is not a monster decorated with wave patterns. Its entire anatomy is the visible zero set of a living eigenfunction.

Heavy fluorescent glycerin, mica powder, black iron filings, and translucent slime migrate across the vibrating membrane into mathematically exact nodal lines. Numerous fully recognizable human eyes physically precipitate only at intersections of those nodal lines: wet acrylic sclera, articulated lids, real lashes, irises vibrating into concentric standing-wave modes, pupils dilating according to local amplitude. Complete human mouths emerge only at saddle points where positive and negative vibration domains exchange stability. Glossy lips pull out of the membrane, mechanical jaws open across the zero contour, wet tongues whip along antinodal trajectories where vibration amplitude is greatest, and recursive teeth become progressively smaller Chladni packing elements near the throat. When the eigenmode changes, old mouths flatten back into anonymous rubber and new mouths begin forming elsewhere.

The practical build is explicit and ultra-realistic: a twelve-foot translucent foam-latex drumhead, concealed subwoofer pistons, eccentric motors, air bladders, magnetic iron powder, pigmented glycerin, cable-operated mouth appliances mounted on sliding tracks, replacement acrylic eyes, high-speed insert photography, undercranked passes, and restrained analog video feedback projected back onto the physical membrane. Color is violent but structured: electric cyan and bubblegum magenta fluid domains divided by absolute black nodal lines, toxic chartreuse eye reflections, highlighter-yellow tongue edges, ultraviolet violet in the deep antinodes. Hard 5600K strobe freezes the wet organs while internal 3200K gelled lamps make the membrane glow from below.

Locked low-angle 35mm camera almost level with the membrane so the vibrating surface becomes a horizonless landscape; one nodal eye is sharp in the foreground, a branching mouth system rises behind it, saturated cyan smoke reveals depth. Pushed 16mm grain beneath VHS noise, phosphor persistence repeats only the fastest vibration, chroma delay leaks magenta and cyan away from the black zero sets, a slow rolling sync bar interferes visibly with the standing-wave bands, mild RF dropout erases one eigenmode near the bottom. The tape damage behaves like a second coupled oscillator but never hides the practical creature.
```

## Image-to-video prompt

```text
Static low-angle camera; no pan, no zoom, no digital motion. Begin with the latex membrane trembling in one stable eigenmode. Fluorescent glycerin and iron powder crawl into black nodal lines under real vibration. Acrylic eyes mechanically rise at nodal intersections and blink in coupled phase clusters. At saddle points, sliding mouth appliances push through the membrane; lips work, jaws bite, tongues whip along antinodal paths. Hidden exciters slowly change frequency, causing a visible mode transition: old nodal lines dissolve, eyes sink, mouths seal, then a different mathematically governed anatomy precipitates elsewhere. Use hard strobe fragments, undercranked insert motion, heavy rubber inertia, speaker-driven ripples, flying droplets, cable lag, and imperfect replacement appliances. The VHS rolling sync bar drifts against the physical wave field, producing brief moiré and phosphor persistence without becoming digital glitch. Preserve cyan/magenta/chartreuse/yellow structured color, 16mm grain, chroma lag, tape stretch, and bottom-edge head switching.
```
